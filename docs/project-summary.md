# OptiStratixAI: Executive summary

Last updated: 2026-09-06

*Written for a non-technical reader.*

---

## The one-line version

OptiStratixAI reads what your competitors publish, works out where you are
losing ground, and turns that into a marketing plan you can actually act on, with a person checking the AI's work at two points along the way.

---

## The problem

A small or medium business can see everything its competitors do. Their website,
their social posts, what customers say about them in reviews and forums. All of
it is public. That is not the hard part.

The hard part is the step in between: turning a pile of unstructured information
into a decision about what to do differently on Monday.

Four things go wrong with the tools that exist today:

1. **The data is unstructured.** There is a lot of it, and reading it properly
   takes time nobody has.

2. **Existing tools do not actually compare.** Scheduling suites like Hootsuite
   report on your own posts. Trend tools like BuzzSumo report on what is popular
   in the category. Neither puts you side by side with the specific businesses
   you are losing customers to.

3. **AI tools give generic advice.** Most produce suggestions that would fit any
   business, which means they fit none. "Post more consistently" is true of
   everyone and useful to no one.

4. **There is no way to check whether the advice is true.** AI systems state
   things confidently whether or not they have grounds to. If you cannot see
   what a recommendation is based on, you cannot judge whether to follow it.

---

## What OptiStratixAI does

It runs a nine-stage process. Seven stages are automated. **Two of them are
yours, and the system will not continue without you.**

**Stage 1: You describe the business.** Your brand, who you sell to, what you
are trying to achieve, and which competitors matter.

**Stage 2: The Scout Agent collects.** It gathers competitor landing pages,
social posts, customer reviews, forum discussions and trade news.

**Stage 3: Everything is organised.** The collected text is cleaned, split into
passages and indexed so it can be searched by meaning rather than keyword.

**Stage 4: The Insight Agent compares.** It works out where you stand against
that material and produces a list of specific gaps, not "improve your content",
but "your closest competitor publishes a weekly guide series that ranks on the
searches your buyers use, and you publish nothing." **Every finding comes with
the exact passage it was drawn from, and a stated confidence level.**

**Stage 5: You review the gaps.** ⬅ *Your first checkpoint.*
Nothing is written until you have been through the list. Keep the gaps that are
real for your business, drop the ones that are not, change any priority the
system got wrong, and leave notes. The system waits.

**Stage 6: Two agents argue.** The Strategist drafts a plan against the gaps
you kept. Then the Critic, a second agent whose job is to disagree, attacks
that draft on four grounds: is it strong, is it clear, is it actually doable,
and is it supported by evidence? It rewrites what is weak and removes what
cannot be justified.

*In practice this catches real problems.* In a typical run, the Strategist
proposes a paid advertising programme; the Critic removes it because nothing in
the intake indicated a budget exists. It cuts a content plan from three articles
a week to one, on the grounds that a plan nobody finishes is worse than a
shorter one that gets done. **You see every one of these changes, with the
original text beside the replacement.**

**Stage 7: You approve the strategy.** ⬅ *Your second checkpoint.*
Read it with the Critic's objections in view. Approve it, or send it back with
notes and get a new version.

**Stage 8: The document.** An approved strategy becomes a full document:
summary, competitive position, prioritised gaps, initiatives, a week-by-week
implementation plan, and the record of what was checked by whom. Exportable as
PDF or Word.

**Stage 9: The content.** Each initiative becomes concrete assets (article
outlines, SEO briefs, social posts, ad copy, email sequences), with the effort
estimated so a quarter can be planned rather than guessed at.

---

## What makes it different

**It compares, rather than describes.** Findings are about the distance between
you and specific named competitors, not a summary of either.

**Every claim carries its source.** Each gap shows the passage it came from,
where that passage was found, and how relevant it was. Nothing is asserted
without something behind it.

**It states its own uncertainty.** Findings carry confidence scores, so a weak
signal looks like a weak signal instead of being presented with the same
confidence as a strong one.

**Two agents, deliberately in tension.** One writes, one attacks. The
disagreements are shown to you, not hidden.

**A person decides.** Twice, and unavoidably. The system cannot write a strategy
without your gap review, and cannot produce a deliverable without your approval.
This is enforced in the software itself, not left to good intentions.

---

## Where the project stands

The complete product is built and running: the public website, the full
application, the database, and all nine stages of the pipeline end to end.

**The collecting is real.** Whichever of the three comparisons you choose, the
system genuinely goes and reads both sides: two marketplace listings (Daraz or
Alibaba), two websites, or two public Instagram profiles. What it finds is
stored, indexed and shown to you.

**The four AI agents that reason over it are currently realistic stand-ins
rather than live AI models.** They produce carefully written, plausible output
so the whole system can be demonstrated without cost, but they are not yet
calling a language model. Everything around them is genuinely built: the process
that stops at your checkpoints, the evidence trail, the storage, the security
model, the document generation. Connecting real agents is a contained,
well-defined next step.

A seeded example, a fictional coffee roaster called Verdant Roast, ships with
the system so the full process can be walked through in a few minutes. Anything
drawn from it is labelled **Demo data**, so nobody is left guessing what is real.
